# Physics DNA

> **Authority:** subordinate to [NESTUDIO_WORLD_BIBLE.md](NESTUDIO_WORLD_BIBLE.md).
> **Companion to:** [VISUAL_DNA.md](VISUAL_DNA.md), [GEOMETRIC_ALPHABET.md](GEOMETRIC_ALPHABET.md),
> [RENDERING_DNA.md](RENDERING_DNA.md).

The **invisible** rules. Visual DNA governs how a Nestudio object *looks*; Physics DNA governs how it
*behaves as matter* — the felt sense that these objects obey a gentler, friendlier universe than ours.
When a form feels wrong but you can't say why, it is almost always a physics violation, not a colour or
lighting one. Every object must feel like it belongs to the **same world** — one where gravity is soft,
mass is inflated, and nothing is ever perfectly straight.

Each law below is stated as **purpose · examples · failure cases · evaluation**.

---

## 1. Soft Gravity

**Purpose.** Objects look *settled*, as if they have gently come to rest — weight without harshness.
Removes the cold, floating, "3D render on a white void" feeling.

**Examples.** A Pebble body widens slightly where it meets its base (it *sits*). A stacked form's lower
element compresses a touch under the one above. Contact with the ground reads as a soft settle, not a
hard landing.

**Failure cases.** Object appears to hover; perfectly rigid stacks with no give; a heavy top on a thin
base with no visual acknowledgement of load; a shape that looks like it would tip.

**Evaluation.** *Does it look like it has weight and has come to rest gently?* If it looks like it is
floating or about to fall, reject.

---

## 2. Internal Inflation

**Purpose.** Every mass feels **filled from the inside** — plump, taut, huggable, never hollow or slack.
This is the single most Nestudio physical quality; it is what makes objects lovable rather than clinical.

**Examples.** A Pebble bulges gently at its belly. A Capsule is a pill under slight internal pressure, not
an extruded cylinder. Corners round *outward* as if air pushes them.

**Failure cases.** Deflated, saggy, or wrinkled surfaces; slab-flat faces; concave sides that read as
empty; anything that looks like a thin shell.

**Evaluation.** *Does it look gently inflated — could you squeeze it?* Flat, hollow or deflated → reject.

---

## 3. Living Asymmetry

**Purpose.** The handcrafted signal. A single artist's hand is present in tiny, intentional irregularities
— nothing is machine-perfect, yet nothing is sloppy. This is what keeps objects from reading as
AI-generated or CAD.

**Examples.** One side of a Pebble a hair fuller than the other; a rim that wavers by a millimetre; a foot
very slightly off-centre. Deviations are **small and calm**, never comedic.

**Failure cases.** Perfect bilateral symmetry (reads as CAD/AI); OR exaggerated wonkiness (reads as
low-quality or "quirky"). Both are wrong — the target is *quiet* imperfection.

**Evaluation.** *Does it feel shaped by a careful hand, not a machine — without looking broken?*

---

## 4. Calm Exaggeration

**Purpose.** Proportions are pushed toward friendliness (chunkier, rounder, more characterful than
reality) but **never** into cartoon or novelty. Desirability over accuracy; restraint over spectacle.

**Examples.** A mug's belly a little fuller than a real mug; a chair's legs a little stubbier and softer;
a tree's canopy a little heavier. The exaggeration is felt, not announced.

**Failure cases.** Photoreal exactness (cold, generic); OR meme/novelty exaggeration (bug-eyes, absurd
proportions, "cute overload"). Nestudio is warm and premium, not zany.

**Evaluation.** *Is it friendlier than reality yet still calm and premium?* If it shouts, dial it back.

---

## 5. Impossible Stability

**Purpose.** Forms may balance in ways real physics wouldn't quite allow — a heavy mass resting serenely
on a slim foot — because Nestudio's world is **kinder** than ours. This creates gentle delight and
enables the pedestal silhouette. It must always read as *effortless calm*, never precarious tension.

**Examples.** A round Pebble body poised confidently on a small tapered Capsule foot. A canopy floating
above a slim trunk. The balance looks **inevitable and restful**, not lucky.

**Failure cases.** Balance that reads as *tension* or *danger* (about to topple); OR over-engineered
supports that kill the lightness. Wrong in both directions.

**Evaluation.** *Does it balance with serene confidence?* Anxious or precarious → reject.

---

## 6. Breathing Negative Space

**Purpose.** Openings, gaps and cut-outs are **first-class shapes**, drawn with the same care as the
solid — calm, generous, rounded. Negative space is where the object *breathes*; crowding it kills the
premium calm. Ties directly to the Arch letter and the M24 cut-out rule.

**Examples.** A handle is a clean rounded aperture with generous margin, not a thin gap. The space *under*
an arch is a deliberate soft shape. Around every object sits generous, even margin.

**Failure cases.** Thin, fiddly, or ragged openings; cut-outs crammed against edges; busy silhouettes with
no room to breathe; decoration filling the calm.

**Evaluation.** *Is the empty space as intentional and calm as the solid?* Cramped or fiddly → reject.

---

## How Physics DNA is used

- **During generation** — the prompts encode these as felt qualities (inflated, soft, settled, single
  radius, generous margin). See [RENDERING_DNA.md](RENDERING_DNA.md) and
  [ART_DIRECTION_PROCESS.md](ART_DIRECTION_PROCESS.md).
- **During review** — the six laws are scored rows on the evaluation scorecard
  ([ART_DIRECTION_PROCESS.md](ART_DIRECTION_PROCESS.md) §Evaluation). An asset can be beautifully rendered
  and still fail physics (e.g. a deflated body, an anxious balance) — such assets are **rejected**.
- **When adding categories or primitives** — the new thing must obey all six laws, or it does not belong
  to the world.
