# 🧪 Icon Tests — the emotional evaluation battery

> The permanent tests for the **soul** of Nestudio — stronger than the object-level [3-second silhouette
> test](GEOMETRIC_ALPHABET.md) and the [asset scorecard](ART_DIRECTION_PROCESS.md), which judge *pieces.*
> These judge the **whole world's ability to make someone wish it were real.** Subordinate to the [World
> Bible](NESTUDIO_WORLD_BIBLE.md); they operationalize the [Soul](SOUL_OF_NESTUDIO.md) and extend the
> [Living Test](LIVING_WORLD.md#chapter-12--the-living-test).
>
> Any world moment, house, scene, or feature can be run through these. Each has a clear **PASS** and **FAIL.**
> A thing may pass every technical gate and still fail here — and if it fails here, it is wrong, because the
> soul outranks the craft.

---

## The master filter (applies to all tests)
> *Nestudio is not a social network with beautiful rooms. It is a place people genuinely wish they could
> visit.* If a thing makes Nestudio more useful but less desirable to visit, it **fails** — no matter its
> test scores.

---

## 1. The Empty World Test
Remove every creator, Nest, UI element and interaction. Leave only sky, ground, light, weather, and the
world's slow breath, at golden hour, with a little rain.
- **PASS** — a person would want to simply *be* there, doing nothing, and feel held.
- **FAIL** — the empty world feels dead, dull, or "unfinished." (Then no feature can save it; fix the world.)

## 2. The Child Drawing Test
A person sees a Nestudio house (or object) once and draws it from memory a day later.
- **PASS** — their drawing is unmistakably Nestudio: the rounded lap-body, the big soft roof-hat, the one
  glowing window, the curl of smoke. (See [House DNA](HOUSE_DNA.md).)
- **FAIL** — their drawing could be any cute house from any game; nothing essential survives memory.

## 3. The Winter Night Test
Show the world at night, in winter, with snow, and nothing happening.
- **PASS** — it is the **safest, warmest, most beautiful** image in Nestudio: the Warm Window a gold beacon,
  lanterns lit, snow hushing the world, and a deep wish to be *inside.* Night is the world's best hour.
- **FAIL** — the night feels cold, lonely, empty, or the slightest bit scary. (A scary Nestudio night is a
  total failure — [Living World, Light](LIVING_WORLD.md#chapter-7--light).)

## 4. The Rain Test
Show the world in rain, from outside a Nest.
- **PASS** — the rain makes the Warm Window glow *warmer* and creates a cozy ache to be indoors; rain reads as
  *comfort and shelter.*
- **FAIL** — the rain reads as gloom, grey, sad, or threatening; it makes a person want to leave.

## 5. The Returning Home Test
A person leaves their Nest and comes back later.
- **PASS** — return creates a specific *exhale of relief and recognition* — the place was waiting, the
  [Glow](THE_CREATOR_SPIRIT.md) knows them, coming back feels *better* than arriving new.
- **FAIL** — return feels like nothing; the place is a static file that neither missed them nor remembers
  them.

## 6. The Screenshot Test *(the three-second test)*
Show one still screenshot to someone who has never heard of Nestudio, for three seconds.
- **PASS** — an involuntary *"I want to be there."* Longing before understanding.
- **FAIL** — "that's a nice app / game / room." Admiration without longing. Admiration is a fail.

## 7. The "I Wish This Existed" Test
Ask the viewer, honestly: do you wish this place were real and you could go?
- **PASS** — a genuine, slightly wistful *yes* — the same yes people feel about Totoro's forest or a Ghibli
  town. They wish they could visit.
- **FAIL** — "it's pretty" or "it's well made." If the answer is about quality and not *desire*, it fails.

## 8. The One Hour Test
A person spends an hour in the world.
- **PASS** — they leave **rested and warmer** than they arrived, having lost track of time in a good way, and
  quietly wanting to come back. (See [Soul](SOUL_OF_NESTUDIO.md) arc.)
- **FAIL** — they leave drained, restless, or with the hollow "why did I just spend an hour on this" feeling
  of a feed. That feeling is the enemy.

## 9. The Friend Test
A person visits a friend's Nest for the first time.
- **PASS** — they feel they *met the person* through their home — "this is so *them*" — a warm curiosity about
  a human being. The Nest is a portrait, not a profile.
- **FAIL** — it feels like browsing a page of someone's stuff; they learn facts, not a feeling. It reads as a
  profile, a portfolio, or a shop.

## 10. The Vacation Test
Ask: is this a place a person would *go to unwind* — not to accomplish anything, just to *be*?
- **PASS** — yes; Nestudio is somewhere you'd visit the way you'd visit a quiet cabin or a favourite café —
  for the *being there*, with no task in mind.
- **FAIL** — it is only worth opening when you have something to *do* (post, check, manage). A place you only
  visit with a purpose is a tool, not a destination.

---

## How to use these tests

- **Run them on everything with a soul dimension** — houses, scenes, weather, arrival, the village, any
  feature that touches the *experience of the place.* (Objects use the silhouette test + scorecard instead.)
- **The soul tests can veto.** A feature can pass every technical review and still be rejected for failing
  the Vacation, Screenshot, or Empty World test. When soul and utility conflict, **soul wins** (the master
  filter).
- **They are diagnostic, not just pass/fail.** A failure points to *where* the world lost its magic —
  usually light, warmth, pacing, restraint, or the loss of the Warm Window — and that is where to fix it.
- **These tests are permanent.** Like the silhouette test before them, they outlive any sprint and any team.
  A designer thirty years from now should be able to hold a new idea up to *"I wish this existed"* and *"the
  Winter Night Test"* and know, instantly, whether it is Nestudio.

> **The battery in one line:** *if a stranger wouldn't ache to visit, a friend's Nest wouldn't feel like the
> friend, and the empty world at a snowy dusk wouldn't feel like the safest place on earth — it isn't
> Nestudio yet.*
