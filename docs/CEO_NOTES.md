# 👤 CEO Notes — who we're building for

> Not philosophy. Practical. This is what **Hannan** (founder, CEO, and the *first real user*) actually
> wants, in his own spirit. Every engineer/AI should re-read this before deciding what to build next.
> Written end of M31; keep it current, don't rewrite it.

---

## What Hannan wants

- **"I want to feel something."** He judges the product by *feeling*, not by architecture, test counts, or
  cleverness. If it doesn't move him, it isn't done — no matter how green the gates are.
- **"I don't want another document."** The documentation phase is over. More philosophy/specs is *not*
  progress to him right now. He wants to *experience* the thing.
- **"I don't care about prettier prompts."** Prompt engineering is a means, never the deliverable. He cares
  about the *result on screen*, not the wording that produced it.
- **"I want the first magical interaction."** One real moment that makes him smile: *my object became part of
  my home, and it was waiting for me when I came back.*
- **"I want to become the first user."** He tests on **his own phone**, through the **visible app UI** — no
  terminals, no dev mode, no hidden routes, no files copied into folders.
- **"I judge by feeling, and I'll send screenshots."** Expect screenshots and gut reactions, not spec
  reviews. Respond by improving the *felt result*, then show him again.

## How to work with him (operating agreement)

- **Show, don't tell.** Deliver a screenshot / a working preview, not a report of intentions.
- **The real render is the judge.** "It technically works" is not success; "it *feels* right" is. (He
  rejected a technically-working slice because the generated mug felt like a photo cutout — see the
  furniture@7 correction.)
- **One object, done properly** beats a hundred generated assets. Fix the mug until it's undeniable, then
  generalize.
- **Human approval is mandatory.** GPT/Gemini creates; **Hannan judges**; Claude records. Nothing auto-ships
  or auto-selects.
- **Stay in scope.** When in doubt, do *less*, and make the one thing better. He'd rather have one magical
  moment than five half-magical features.
- **Mobile-first, always.** If it doesn't work beautifully on his iPhone through the normal UI, it doesn't
  count.

## The one test he applies

> **"Does this now look like my real thing was rebuilt by Nestudio's own artists — and do I *want to keep
> it*?"**

If yes, ship it to preview and let him feel it. If no, iterate the render — not the docs.
